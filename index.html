Parts

Part numbers do not travel. Describe what a part is and it can be compared with everything else you buy, whatever it is called. Anything you leave blank stays unknown — it never counts as a match.

Describe a part

What should this part cost?

The finished part's weight is not the question. Between it and the stock you buy sit a bigger blank, a saw that turns metal into dust, a sheet that does not divide evenly, scrap at every operation and a supplier who sells sheets rather than square millimetres. This works out what to order, how much, and why — every quantity separately, so a challenge can be answered.

What this release does, and what it does not

Everything is entered by hand. There is no document extraction in this build — nothing on this page reads a PDF, a photograph or a CAD file. A 2D drawing does not contain recoverable 3D geometry, and no dimension or certificate value here was recovered from one.

Everything you record stays in this browser. Nothing is uploaded, pooled with anybody else's records, or published.

Your scenarios

1 · The part
Blank — what is released into production
Finished part — optional, and only where the geometry is this simple

Leave these empty unless the part really is a plain rectangle. A net mass computed from a shape the part does not have is worse than no net mass.

2 · Density

Mass and therefore material cost rest on this. It depends on grade, condition and specification revision — it is not implied by an alloy name, so nothing here supplies one for you.

3 · The stock you can buy

Leave that unticked where grain direction, finish or a drawing note fixes the orientation. Nothing here will turn a part you did not say could be turned.

4 · The route

A yield is accepted output divided by input. It is not a scrap percentage added to demand: 100 parts at 95% yield needs 106 blanks, not 105, and across a route the difference compounds. Setup and test pieces must say which side of the yield they come from — that is the mistake that counts the same pieces twice.

Engineering requirements
no model needed

Tolerances, finishes and specifications, for the whole part or for one feature. Record them now — none of this waits for a drawing or a model. Naming a specification records the citation, not its contents: nothing here knows what any standard requires, so paste the clause you are working from if you have it.

The limits — state them one way, not two
Where it comes from
5 · The cost

Fill in what you have. Leave an amount blank and it stays blank — it becomes a declared gap that blocks the total and says what it needs, rather than a zero that reads as free. An element that does not apply to this part, leave switched off entirely.

6 · Contingency

Extra blanks above what the route requires, kept as a separate line with its own name. Material quietly inflated to be safe cannot be told apart from material planning that is wrong, and neither can be defended.

Send for technical review

What a certificate check is, and is not

This compares the values written on a certificate against the requirements written in a specification, a drawing and a purchase order. That is a comparison of two documents. It does not establish that the material is what the certificate says it is, that it is in the stated condition, that the right heat arrived, or that any of it is fit for the job — and nothing here can release material for manufacture or installation.

No limit is supplied for you. This has no opinion about what any grade requires: every requirement below comes from a document you identify by revision and clause, because revisions change limits.

1 · The certificate
Three identities, not one

A distributor's paperwork error is not a mill's. Where the certificate does not name the producer, leave it blank — the result will say the lot cannot be attributed rather than crediting whoever's letterhead it is on.

The material
2 · What the certificate reports

Enter each value exactly as it is written, inequality symbols and trailing zeros included. <0.005 is not 0.005, and 0.03 is not 0.030 — both differences change the answer. Tick a row only once you have checked it against the document.

3 · What the documents require

Each limit carries the document and the exact revision it came from. Where two documents ask for different things, both are checked and the conflict is reported — picking the tighter one looks responsible and is still a decision about somebody's drawing that nobody authorised.

What this is, and what it is not

A record of how reviewed lots turned out. It is not a probability that the next delivery conforms, it is not a supplier rating, and there is no single score per mill — one number would hide the sample size, pool different specifications as though they were the same requirement, and put a mill with two good lots above one with two hundred.

Two out of two and two hundred out of two hundred are both 100%. Below the evidence threshold no percentage is shown at all — not one with a caveat beside it, because the figure is what gets carried away. The threshold is a product rule, not a statistical guarantee.

Why this project exists

BuyrWorld exists because procurement tooling has been stuck for a decade: clunky suites built for auditors, or generic AI that's never seen an RFQ. We're building the platform buyers deserve — modern, fast and genuinely useful on a Tuesday afternoon when three quotes land at once.

The project is maintained independently as procurement technology research. It is not staffed, not incorporated as a service, and not accepting commercial work. Methodology and calculations are published so they can be inspected rather than taken on trust.
